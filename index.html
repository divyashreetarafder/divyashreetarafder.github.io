Automatic transfer switches, keeping critical power alive

The automatic transfer switch is the reflex that keeps a hospital or data centre lit: sense the grid failing, start the generator, and move the load across before anyone notices. My range covered the specification, the standards and the launch. Cut the grid and watch the transfer.

Grid healthy, load supplied

The load runs on the grid. Cut the supply and watch the switch move the load to the generator. That transfer time is the difference between a hospital going dark and staying lit.

  • Open-transition transfer: break from the dead source before making to the live one, so the two supplies never touch.
  • Sensing → generator start → transfer, all inside the switch's rated time, the number a hospital's backup plan is built around.
  • ASCO Group 5 controller over MODBUS, so a facility can see and command every switch from its building-management system.

Research

Research & publications

Two indexed publications from my undergraduate work. Both deal with faults that stay hidden until they do real damage, one in motor-drive bearings and one in buried cable.

01

Circulating current & common-mode voltage in an inverter-fed induction motor, using PID and fuzzy control

A variable-speed drive switches so fast that the three phase voltages no longer sum to zero, that leftover "common-mode" voltage pushes small currents through the motor's bearings and slowly pits them until the bearing fails. I compared a conventional PID controller against a hybrid PID–fuzzy scheme and cut the common-mode voltage by 94.6%, the shaft voltage by 85.6% and the bearing current by 66.7%.

Solid State Technology · vol. 63(3), 2020 · indexed
02

Underground cable fault detection using the Internet of Things

Finding a fault in buried cable is usually a dig-and-guess job. By reading how the circuit changes with distance and streaming it to the cloud, the fault becomes a coordinate, located to within about ±100 m, so a crew digs once, in the right place.

Cognitive Informatics & Soft Computing (AISC 1317), Springer · 2021 · indexed · doi:10.1007/978-981-16-1056-1_66
